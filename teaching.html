Teaching & supervision

Learning by doing.

Connecting the foundations of vision and robotics with practical systems.

Machine Vision B31MV · MSc

When: Every spring semester
Weekly teaching: 3 hours of lectures and 2 hours of labs.

B31MV takes a relatively high-level, vision-systems perspective, with an emphasis on how images are used for perception, learning and decision-making, rather than focusing heavily on the underlying signal-processing theory.

Open Canvas ↗ for course materials, announcements and timetable information (Heriot-Watt login required).

Weekly outline

WeekTopics
1Introduction to human, machine, computer and robotic vision, with relevant applications.
2Image formation: camera models, projections and image representations.
3Basic image processing: convolution, filtering, segmentation, edge detection and image interpolation.
4Feature extraction and matching; feature detectors and descriptors; K-means clustering; visual codebooks and Bag of Visual Words; applications to object detection, image classification and visual search.
5Supervised learning, training/validation/test splits, linear regression and regularisation.
6Consolidation Week (Learning Break)
7Neural networks and convolutional neural networks.
83D vision, camera calibration and stereo triangulation.
9Structured light, structure from motion and photometric stereo.
10RANSAC, Hough transforms, visual servoing and robotics.
11–12Revision and practice, including mock exams.

Syllabus

Learning activityDetailsLearning outcomes
Lectures
  • Introduction: human, machine, computer and robotic vision, and applications.
  • Image formation: camera models, projections and image representations.
  • Basic image processing: convolution, filtering, segmentation, edge detection and image interpolation.
  • Feature extraction and matching: feature detectors and descriptors; unsupervised clustering (K-means); visual codebooks; Bag of Visual Words; applications to object detection, image classification and visual search.
  • 3D imaging: applications, laser and stereo triangulation, structured light, structure from motion and photometric stereo.
  • Visual servoing and applications of vision for robotic control.
  • Tracking: templates and cross-correlation; blob trackers.
  • Machine learning: deep learning models for image/video recognition and classification, data preparation and model validation.

Introduction to Robotics Undergraduate labs

I contribute to practical robotics teaching through hands-on laboratory sessions using Niryo Ned2 robots. Students explore robot operation and manipulation, including vision and conveyor-based tasks.

Student projects and supervision

I supervise MSc projects and PhD research in robotic perception, learning and manipulation. Project areas include medical robotics, precision optical assembly, adaptive disassembly and vision-based robot learning.

If you are interested in a project, email me with a brief description of your interests and background. Information about our PhD researchers is available on the ARM²Lab website.

Short courses

I have also delivered a compact robotics course in Xi’an, covering robotics fundamentals, ROS, Python, motion and manipulation, machine learning and autonomous robots.
